Requesting a new public trashcan, explained

Pick a busy spot on a main street

The city puts cans on main roads with lots of pedestrians, where people walk their dogs, wait for the bus or come out of a store. Pruitt said they’re generally very skeptical of side streets unless there’s a specific reason, such as a corner store or a bus stop.

Bus stops are a good bet

Pruitt called RTA stops easy wins. RTA places shelters where there are lots of riders. There’s room for more. According to Signal’s analysis, only 12.9% of the city’s 2,338 GCRTA stops have a public can within 100 feet, and 1,509 stops have no can nearby at all.

Be specific about where

The city generally places the can where the requester asks. “They tell you where you want it... we just kind of follow their lead,” Pruitt said. Name the corner or side of the street, and explain who will use it: people getting off the bus, kids walking to school, workers heading to a parking lot.

Need a surface to bolt to

The main obstacle is having no surface to bolt to. For example, the city received requests for outside the Carnegie library in Ohio City, which is surrounded by grass. They only have a handful of “composite” cans with bottoms filled with sand that don’t need to be bolted to concrete.
